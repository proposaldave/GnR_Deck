# Phone Call Learning Engine

This is the simple version of the strategy.

Give n Receive is not just trying to send better invitations. The deeper opportunity is to learn what people want, who they want it with, when they can do it, and what would make them feel genuinely connected to the club. A text can invite someone. A phone call can discover the truth.

The key insight is that people like warm invitations. They like getting a text that feels personal. They wait for it. They are excited when the right invitation comes. But the next level is a scheduled human call: "Want me to call you while you are in the car for five minutes so we can figure out what you are looking for?"

That call is not just customer service. It is the first version of autonomous social coordination: the system learns enough from real human conversation to recommend the next person to call, the next question to ask, and the next thing to schedule.

The person gets a human connection experience. NEPC learns what they actually want. Give n Receive captures the signal. Then the next invitation, event, lesson, class, clinic, membership conversation, or referral ask gets smarter.

## The Loop

1. Invite

Start with a warm text or direct invitation. If the person is busy, offer a timed call. The call is framed as helpful: "I am trying to understand what would actually be fun and useful for you, not just blast you with random events."

2. Listen

The call asks the questions the software cannot guess:

- What are you looking for right now?
- Are you trying to play more, learn more, meet people, or get more comfortable?
- What days and times actually work?
- Who have you enjoyed playing with?
- Who would make a group feel fun?
- Are you interested in a lesson, clinic, social play, glow event, private party, membership, or something else?
- Is there anyone you would want to bring or introduce to the club?

3. Coordinate

If the right event, lesson, or group already exists, schedule it immediately or send the next step. If it does not exist yet, collect the demand and wait for center mass: enough compatible people, with compatible times, to create the right session.

4. Learn

Every call becomes a data point:

- scheduling availability
- lesson demand
- class and clinic interest
- preferred level and vibe
- social connection signal
- referral names
- membership interest
- experience gaps
- follow-up promises

The goal is not to collect data for its own sake. The goal is to make the next human action better.

5. Improve

After each batch of calls, the AI reviews the transcripts and improves:

- who to call next
- what opener worked
- what questions produced the best signal
- what event should be created next
- what membership objection needs to be handled
- what referrals should be asked for
- which players should be grouped together
- what the front desk or pros should follow up on

The protocol gets sharper with each call.

## The Machine

The machine we are building is simple:

1. Pick the highest-value person to call.
2. Give Dave the exact opener and first question.
3. Listen for tone, excitement, hesitation, objections, and hidden demand.
4. Suggest the next question during or immediately after the call.
5. Turn the call into one approved next action.
6. Improve the next call queue.

Inputs:

- Quo call summary or transcript
- event waitlists and empty spots
- lesson/class/clinic demand
- member/non-member status
- beginner/top-of-funnel status
- referral hints
- prior attendance and no-show signals
- human-verified scheduling windows

Outputs:

- next person to call
- why this person matters now
- first question to ask
- likely offer to make
- follow-up text
- schedule/referral/membership action
- one-line memory candidate for future use

The machine does not auto-book. It does not invent lesson times. It does not expose sensitive notes to players. It helps the human operator act faster, warmer, and smarter.

## The First Call Queues

Do not make warm outreach calls from a number still showing as spam risk. First, get Quo deliverability clean enough that the call feels legitimate.

Once the number is safe, the first queues are:

1. Beginner/top-of-funnel players tied to this week's beginner social or recent beginner experiences.
2. Middleton members who can pressure-test referrals, private parties, and member-led intro nights.
3. People with lesson demand who are waiting for Dave or another pro.
4. Happy followers from Dave-led events who can name friends, times, and groups they would actually enjoy.

The first question is not "Do you want to buy a membership?" The first question is:

> I am trying to understand what would make NEPC more useful and fun for you. Are you mainly looking to play more, learn more, meet people, bring friends, or figure out whether membership makes sense?

Then branch:

- If they want to play more: ask when, level, and who makes it fun.
- If they want to learn more: ask lesson length, clinic topic, and preferred pro.
- If they want connection: ask what kind of group would make them excited to come back.
- If they mention friends: ask for one intro text, not a vague referral.
- If membership comes up: ask what they understand, what is unclear, and what would make it obviously worth it.

The goal of every call is one clean next action.

The call should feel generous, not extractive.
